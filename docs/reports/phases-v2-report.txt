== 1. COUNTER-PRESS THAT HAPPENS (item 1)
Cause: the counter-press acted only on a carried ball (most losses leave the ball loose or in
flight), ranked every outfielder including the rest defence, and competed with marking on a
near coin flip (press about 0.74 v mark about 0.7 at softmax temperature 0.05).
Fix: the plan's counterpressPlayers are picked at the moment of loss by time to the ball, the
two deepest men goal-side of it stay as rest defence, and they react to the loss as a drilled
trigger; the set is re-read whenever the ball changes feet or is played, so the press follows
the ball for counterpressSec, then the side settles into its block. New job `counterpress`:
first man closes the carrier goal-side and brakes into him, second cuts his escape, the rest go
tight on the carrier's short options; on a pass out of the press they press the receiver; on a
loose ball the first two go to it. A counter-presser values winning the ball back higher
(opponent unorganised), so he goes in on a closer call. onLoss=recover: men caught upfield of
the ball value getting back into the block above marking (0.75 v 0.3) and sprint.

Metric (open-play losses, the engine's defensive_transition entry, play still live 1.5 s
later): loser's outfielders within 10 m of the ball 1.5 s after the loss; regained in open
play within 6 s. MODE=cp = same sides and ratings, one manager counter-presses 4 for 5 s, the

== 2. RED CARDS BY LAST-MAN BEHAVIOUR (item 2)
Cause (scripts/red-diag.ts, 40 to 60 std matches): 23 of 36 reds were TACTICAL lunges from
behind by the last man on a carrier breaking at goal: the professional-foul decision priced a
free kick and a booking, never the red it would be, nor what the defence could still do.
Fix: if taking the man would be DOGSO (outside the box, inside 28 m, no team-mate goal-side,
keeper not covering) the foul costs the red card (a man down for the rest of the match, less of
it late on), and the break is only worth stopping for what the defence cannot otherwise take
away: recoverShare compares the carrier's time to shooting range with the quickest cover's time
to get goal-side (a team-mate, the defender himself, or the keeper coming out to narrow, which
counts for less). Judgement from what he sees; no rolls. Counter-pressers brake into the
carrier (no charging through him).

== 3. OUT-OF-POSSESSION COMPACTNESS (item 3)
Cause (scripts/block-extent.ts): forwards set the block's top 81% of the time, 30+ m ahead of
the back two: their fallback marking was anchored to the old formation anchor deep in the
opponent's half (marking centre-backs), and they walked back into the block.
Fix: blockPoint honours blockLength as the whole unit (midfield 0.32 of it ahead of the back
line, forwards 0.55; one outlet at blockLength - 8 only for managers who counter on a regain);
a forward's fallback marking is anchored to his block station; a man ahead of the block runs
back in; an untriggered carrier beyond the block's reach is screened from the top of the block.

== 4. GOALS, SAVES, FOULS (item 4)
Where goals came from (scripts/goal-origin.ts, parry-goals.ts, shot-phase.ts): a quarter of
goals (0.68 a match) were the keeper's own parry going in: he always turned his palm to the
post nearer the contact point, so a ball near the middle of the goal was pushed toward a post
it could not reach. He now turns the palm the way his intended deflection clears the goal by
more: round a post or over the bar first, back out in front of goal second. Parries into the
net 0.68 to about 0.1 a match. Counter-pressers no longer stand in the passing lanes (that cut
out passes in flight: interceptions reached 60 a match); they go tight on the short options.
Against a compact block the front line's midfielders and wide men arrive at the D (18 m) rather
than 24 m out (strikers still pin the line).
Keeper claims: 23 to 20.4 a match at 6.6 s each (2.3 dead minutes); they are not the
dead-time problem. Free kicks (19 a match, 31.7 s each, 10.1 min) and throw-ins (41, 15.1 s,
10.2 min) are. Ball in play 53.2 to 57.8 min. Fouls 16.6 to 16.9: most fouls are body
charges (about 10 a match); lunges at the ball almost never meet the man first, which is why
fouls stay under 18 even with ball in play back in its 55-60 band.
